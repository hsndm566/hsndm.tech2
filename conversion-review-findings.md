# AutoApply SA Conversion Review — Live Journey

## Current strengths

The live landing page makes the Saudi Arabia-only positioning clear, shows a direct Jeddah anchor, carries a clear campaign CTA above the fold, presents 99 SAR entry pricing, and provides an interactive client-side CV-matching preview. Visitors can select city, industry, seniority, and language before choosing a CV. The page then explains the campaign workflow, offers an illustrative process case study, lists monthly plans, provides three user-supplied reviews, addresses common concerns, and offers a direct WhatsApp fallback.

## Main conversion gap

The current CV interaction is a useful preview but it stops before delivering a durable, concrete personal outcome. A first-time visitor can see a match result in the browser, but cannot leave with a reusable campaign brief, example tailored headline, readiness score, or clearly bounded free deliverable. The paid service is comprehensible, but the bridge between "I understand the engine" and "I trust it with my job search" could be stronger.

## Trial-design guardrails

Any trial should avoid promising real applications, guaranteed interviews, or fabricated role availability. A useful low-risk trial can instead deliver a transparent diagnostic or campaign-preview artifact: profile signals detected locally, target role lanes, missing profile inputs, and the next campaign step. It should explicitly state that no applications are sent and no CV file leaves the browser unless the visitor chooses WhatsApp or the enquiry flow.

## Research patterns to adapt

Career.io places a free resume review ahead of its fuller job-search offer and promises an actionable ranking-style assessment rather than an employment outcome. Indeed Career Scout leads with personalisation: career-path exploration, role matching, resume customisation, and feedback improve as a candidate supplies more profile context. The Ladders makes the free review concrete by stating the exact diagnostic components: resume quality, ATS analysis, actionable feedback, and keyword analysis. Together, these patterns support a transparent AutoApply SA "Campaign Readiness Check" rather than a generic free trial.

## Recommended trial framing

The strongest candidate is a no-card, no-application "Saudi Campaign Readiness Check". It should take roughly one minute, use the existing client-side CV matching and Saudi preference controls, and deliver four bounded outputs: 2–3 detected role lanes, a CV-readiness checklist, target-profile completeness, and a sample campaign brief. The completion screen should give a clear choice: save or share the brief on WhatsApp to begin a paid campaign. It should explicitly say that the check does not apply to roles, reserve jobs, or guarantee an interview.
